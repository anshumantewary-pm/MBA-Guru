# Trust and cash, not matching, break Indian trucking

India's trucking problem is mainly about trust and working capital, not a shortage of trucks or of matching apps. About **$170 bn a year** of road freight still moves **mostly offline through layered brokers**, and digital platforms handle **under 2%** of it ([Redseer](https://redseer.com/articles/unlocking-growth-in-indias-fragmented-trucking-sector/)). Supply is spread across roughly 3.5 million operators, three-quarters of whom own fewer than five trucks. The friction falls on both sides. Shippers, especially MSMEs, face opaque prices: one Delhi–Guwahati load can cost anywhere from ₹85,000 to ₹1.5 lakh. Truck owners wait 24–48 hours between loads, run only 18–20 days a month, and pay fuel and tolls upfront while waiting 45–90 days for payment that depends on a paper proof of delivery (POD). Drivers are scarce, at roughly 55–75 per 100 trucks, underpaid and in poor health. Infrastructure delay has moved from toll plazas and state borders to factory gates and warehouse yards. The platforms that have made money (BlackBuck in toll and fuel payments, Porter in intra-city delivery) avoided intercity load matching, while asset-heavy and marketplace models stalled or collapsed. The biggest open gaps sit where trust and money meet: guaranteed payment released against a digital POD, working-capital credit for 1–10 truck owners, verified counterparties, procurement tools for mid-size and small shippers, and enforceable detention charges.

## Shippers pay up to 76% more for the same lane

Finding a truck in India still mostly means a phone call to a broker at a transport nagar, the truck hubs found in most cities. Redseer's industry report for BlackBuck's IPO describes how matching works: load details "are written on paper and matched with truck operators over the phone or in person". Matching is hard because a hub has no shared view of available loads and trucks, because many variables must line up (body type, tonnage, floor, dimensions, commodity, timing, the driver's preferred lanes and language), and because of a "lack of trust in working with an unknown shipper/truck operator" ([BlackBuck/Redseer Industry Report](https://bb-website-public-assets.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/legal-assets/pdf/Industry+Report.pdf)). Broker commissions are poorly measured. The same report gives both **3–8%** and **8–10%**, and platform blogs say **8–15% per layer**, rising to 20–25% when two brokers are in the chain ([TruckGuru](https://truckguru.co.in/blog/?p=2848)). No source measures what share of loads goes through brokers. Every source simply says "most".

The cost of these layers shows clearly in the government's first official logistics-cost study. On Delhi–Guwahati, an 18-tonne truck booked directly with a transporter that has a return load costs **₹85,000**. A brokered truck with no return load costs **up to ₹1.1 lakh**. An MSME shipping part-loads through consolidators pays **₹1.4–1.5 lakh per truck-equivalent**, 65–76% above the best rate. The study concludes that pricing is "less about distance and more about network leverage, market timing, and relationship management" ([DPIIT–NCAER 2025](https://www.dpiit.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/09/c0d7c33f0b737ed71728ff6c2a35f1e7.pdf)). Firm size compounds the gap: logistics costs **16.9% of output for firms under ₹5 crore turnover, against 7.6% for firms over ₹250 crore**. India's total logistics cost is 7.97% of GDP, well below the long-quoted 13–14% ([DPIIT–NCAER 2025](https://www.dpiit.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/09/c0d7c33f0b737ed71728ff6c2a35f1e7.pdf); [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/economy-and-policy/indias-logistics-cost-pegged-at-797-pc-of-gdp-in-first-systematic-study)). Indian logistics is therefore not uniformly expensive. It is cheap for large shippers on contract and expensive for the long tail.

Availability problems are local and seasonal rather than national. Truck rentals fell 3–7% through mid-2025 on oversupply ([Autocar Pro](https://www.autocarpro.in/news/truck-rentals-drop-5-7-in-may-on-key-routes-as-supply-exceeds-demand-126736?amp=1)). Crisil's freight index stayed almost flat into April 2026 and cited "relatively high fleet availability" ([Crisil](https://www.crisil.com/content/dam/crisil-intelligence/what-we-think/reports/2026/05/crisil-freight-index-dips-in-april.pdf)). Peaks still hurt. Ludhiana–Ahmedabad rates rise **30–40% at Diwali**, freight costs climb **up to 200% over Christmas and New Year**, the March–June perishables season tightens supply, and the monsoon adds 25–40% to trip times. Shippers sending goods in the "headhaul" direction, such as from an industrial hub to a Tier-2/3 town, pay for the empty return leg, and return cargo is priced 30–40% below outbound ([DPIIT–NCAER 2025](https://www.dpiit.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/09/c0d7c33f0b737ed71728ff6c2a35f1e7.pdf)). Delhi NCR's night-only entry for heavy trucks, and rules in parts of the northeast that require drivers from local communities, create further pockets of shortage ([DPIIT–NCAER 2025](https://www.dpiit.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/09/c0d7c33f0b737ed71728ff6c2a35f1e7.pdf)).

Once goods are loaded, shippers have little visibility and little recourse. Carriers' statutory liability is capped at **10 times the freight charge**, so ₹6 lakh of goods on a ₹3,000 freight bill carries just ₹30,000 of carrier exposure. Standard transit cover often excludes pilferage ([Zane Marketing](https://zane.marketing/ecommerce-inventory-transit-insurance-india/); [FleetCodes](https://www.fleetcodes.com/blog/cargo-insurance-indian-transporters-guide-2026/)). Turnaround slips because of yard congestion: one Mumbai manufacturer averages 2.5 days against an expected 1.5, and drivers "rely on verbal communication" for status updates ([DPIIT–NCAER 2025](https://www.dpiit.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/09/c0d7c33f0b737ed71728ff6c2a35f1e7.pdf)). On intra-city apps, users complain of fares doubling after the driver arrives and of damage claims being refused ([Voxya](https://voxya.com/consumer-complaints/extra-fair-charged/236256); [ConsumerComplaints.in](https://www.consumercomplaints.in/bycompany/porter-a602206.html)). MSMEs surveyed by Borzo named high delivery cost (59.2%) and inconsistent schedules (21.2%) as their main problems ([SMEStreet](https://smestreet.in/sectors/borzo-study-reveals-key-challenges-faced-by-msmes-in-india-9000313)). There is no India-specific data on on-time-in-full rates, real-time tracking adoption, or how long claims take to settle, so the size of the reliability gap is felt but not measured.

## Truck owners lose a third of their time waiting for loads and at the dock

The supply side mirrors the shipper's problem. India has about **12.5 million trucks and 3.5 million operators**, roughly 3.6 trucks per operator ([Redseer](https://redseer.com/articles/unlocking-growth-in-indias-fragmented-trucking-sector/)). The widely repeated "75% own fewer than five trucks" figure refers to operators, not trucks. CRISIL found small operators own under a quarter of heavy trucks above 12 tonnes ([CRISIL](https://www.crisil.com/en/home/our-analysis/reports/2018/11/road-turns-rough-for-small-fleet-operators.html)), while Deloitte says they own about 75% of all trucks ([Deloitte](https://www2.deloitte.com/in/en/pages/consumer-business/articles/road-logistics-india/press-release.html)). Either way, a typical owner has no sales team and depends on commission agents, whom a government white paper calls a "huge" dependency ([Startup India](https://www.startupindia.gov.in/content/dam/invest-india/Templates/public/White%20Paper_Logistics%20Sector.pdf)).

Utilisation is where owners lose money. Trucks wait **24–48 hours** for the next load and run only **18–20 days a month** ([Redseer](https://redseer.com/articles/unlocking-growth-in-indias-fragmented-trucking-sector/)). KPMG estimates **25–30% of return trips run empty**, and congestion and loading delays add 1–2 days per trip. Indian trucks average **250–300 km a day, against 700–800 km in the US and over 500 km in China**, and each 10-point gain in utilisation could cut long-haul rates by 6–7% ([KPMG India](https://kpmg.com/in/en/blogs/2026/01/logistics-costs-to-gdp-can-budget-2026-finally-move-the-needle.html)). Older NITI Aayog work put empty running as high as 40% ([NITI Aayog](https://www.niti.gov.in/sites/default/files/2021-06/FreightReportNationalLevel.pdf)). Put together, these figures suggest a small-fleet truck spends roughly a third of its available time earning nothing. That is an estimate, not a measured figure.

Detention, meaning time spent waiting at loading and unloading points, is the hidden tax. Industry norms allow a free window of 2–4 hours and then about ₹200–500 per hour ([TruckGuru](https://truckguru.co.in/blog/?p=1867)). In practice, owners often get ₹1,000–1,500 a day, "barely enough to cover fixed costs". Much waiting goes unpaid and is "silently borne by drivers and truck owners who have no recourse" ([India Sea Trade News](https://indiaseatradenews.com/?p=106977)). Detention disputes persist because neither side has reliable timestamps ([Fretron](https://www.fretron.com/blog/glossary-detention-demurrage/)). A government study found commercial vehicles average only **37–38 km/h on highways**, and truckers often spend **up to a day each on loading and unloading** ([NewsBytes](https://www.newsbytesapp.com/news/business/trucking-delays-on-highways-prompt-government-intervention/story)). The study's title and date have not been confirmed.

Margins are thin and owners cannot set prices. Fuel and tolls make up about **80% of operating cost** ([Redseer](https://redseer.com/articles/unlocking-growth-in-indias-fragmented-trucking-sector/)). After the 2026 diesel hikes, transporters managed only 3–4% freight increases ([Kotak Neo](https://www.kotakneo.com/news/market-news/freight-rates-raised-4-percent-rising-transport-costs/)). In July 2026, AIMTC (the national transporters' body) warned that freight rates were "largely unchanged" while diesel, tolls, insurance, tyres, AdBlue and compliance costs rose, and that "thousands of small transport operators and self-driving truck owners will be forced to shut down" ([Free Press Journal](https://freepressjournal.in/mumbai/small-transport-operators-face-financial-crisis-as-rising-costs-and-stagnant-freight-rates-threaten-supply-chain-aimtc)). Owners face two pressures at once: too many trucks competing on spot rates, and cost shocks they can only partly pass on.

## Fraud runs both ways and keeps brokers in business

Lack of trust is the main reason brokers keep their position. Indian police records show a steady run of advance-fee frauds. In one case a man was arrested for running a scam through the Vahak load app, asking a shipper for a ₹15,000 advance. In another, a pair posed as established logistics firms to collect booking advances in Delhi in September 2026 ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/india/two-arrested-for-duping-people-with-fake-logistics-services-in-delhi/3058108/)). A Panchkula rice trader lost ₹1.99 lakh trying to book a truck online ([Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/chandigarh/panchkula-businessman-looking-to-transport-rice-to-kerala-duped-of-rs-1-99-lakh-online/amp)). In Indore, fraudsters paid a genuine transporter's driver a ₹5,000 token so that a ₹25 lakh theft of tiles looked legitimate ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/indore/5000-token-made-scam-look-real-indore-tile-trader-loses-25-lakh-in-whatsapp-fraud)). Larger frauds involve whole firms: a Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar transporter lost ₹28.47 lakh to a gang using fake companies ([Lokmat Times](https://www.lokmattimes.com/aurangabad/fake-firms-cheat-transport-company)), and Unizen Logistics filed an FIR over ₹5.74 crore of bounced cheques from a freight partner ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-ulr-freight-directors-booked-for-allegedly-cheating-unizen-logistics-of-574-crore-in-payments)).

Shippers fear fake transporters, and owners fear fake loads and non-payment. Open load boards with weak identity checks attract both, which is why Vahak advertises fraud prevention as a core feature ([exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/advertising-news/vahak-drives-home-the-importance-of-financial-security-for-truckers-in-new-campaign-125885.html)) and why BlackBuck reviewers complain of "fake load availability" ([PissedConsumer](https://blackbuck.pissedconsumer.com/review.html)). A broker sells more than a match. A broker vouches for both sides, extends credit, settles disputes and knows where return loads are. Deloitte found truckers, transporters and retailers all describe a trust deficit kept in place by dense networks of middlemen ([Deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/content/dam/assets-shared/legacy/docs/insights/2022/DI_A-time-of-reckoning.pdf)). Nobody publishes aggregate fraud losses, and NCRB crime data does not separate out load-board fraud. That absence is itself a gap.

## Paper POD holds up a 45–90 day cash gap

The payment chain is where small fleets most often fail. Owners pay for diesel, tolls and driver allowances within days, often in cash, while shippers pay **45–90 days later** ([Forbes Finance Council](https://www.forbes.com/councils/forbesfinancecouncil/2025/10/28/payments-infrastructure-for-trucking-is-an-overlooked-opportunity-in-india/); [InvoiceFollowups](https://invoicefollowups.com/blog/invoice-factoring-for-logistics-and-transport-india)). The best-sourced figure is 40–45 days, counted from when the original paper POD reaches the shipper ([Motor India](https://www.motorindiaonline.in/freeing-up-working-capital-through-smart-fuel-filling/)). An Autocar Pro columnist argues the gap between weekly fuel bills and 30–60 day receivables "has broken more small fleets than any single price hike" ([Autocar Pro](https://www.autocarpro.in/opinion-column/indias-commercial-vehicle-segment-runs-on-fuel-and-guesswork-134142)). This column was written by a fuel-delivery vendor. Brokers and fuel pumps fill the gap with informal credit, which keeps owners tied to those intermediaries ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/councils/forbesfinancecouncil/2025/10/28/payments-infrastructure-for-trucking-is-an-overlooked-opportunity-in-india/)).

The POD connects payments, disputes and insurance. A shipper can hold an entire bill over one faulty POD. Weak PODs leave transporters unable to contest shortage or damage deductions ([Autoplant](https://github.com/autoplantt-art/autoplant/issues/5)). Invoices without a POD usually cannot be discounted for an advance ([InvoiceFollowups](https://invoicefollowups.com/blog/invoice-factoring-for-logistics-and-transport-india)). On the insurance side, a consignee's "clean" POD signature is a standard reason to reject a goods-in-transit claim ([Policybazaar](https://www.policybazaar.com/corporate-insurance/articles/navigating-transit-challenges-for-small-manufacturers/)). Because transit cover is optional and often missing, losses are settled by cutting the freight payment, so the small transporter ends up acting as the insurer. These disputes extend to large contracts: south Indian LPG transporters struck over about **₹50 crore** of pending dues, five years of unpaid toll reimbursements, and unilateral deductions in a new Indian Oil contract ([Whalesbook](https://www.whalesbook.com/news/English/energy/Indian-Oil-LPG-Deliveries-Halted-by-Trucker-Strike-Over-Payments/69eefa575a43f6b807bf0107)). The law gives little protection. Section 43B(h) of the Income Tax Act enforces a 45-day payment rule only for Udyam-registered micro and small enterprises, and many truckers are unregistered or contract through a broker rather than the shipper ([Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/analysis-practical-insights-into-section-43bh-regarding-disallowance-of-delayed-payments-to-mses/)).

Credit is expensive and getting tighter. NBFC truck loans cost roughly **11–18%**, against 9–13% from banks for established operators, and first-time and used-truck buyers pay the most ([Drivio Trucks](https://www.driviotrucks.com/news/truck-financing-in-india-2026-emi-down-payment-eligibility-explained)). In July 2025 Kotak flagged early commercial-vehicle loan stress, sharpest among **operators with fewer than 10 vehicles** ([91Trucks](https://www.91trucks.com/news/cv-loan-stress-rises-amid-slowdown-lenders-hope-for-recovery)). India Ratings expects CV asset quality to come under pressure in H1 FY27, with first-time buyers most vulnerable ([TrucksBuses](https://www.trucksbuses.com/news/cv-loan-asset-quality-may-weaken-in-fy27-as-utilisation-moderates-ind-ra)). Redseer describes the **$54–56 bn used-CV finance market** as dominated by unorganised high-interest lenders ([Redseer](https://redseer.com/articles/unlocking-growth-in-indias-fragmented-trucking-sector/)). Working-capital products such as invoice discounting and fuel credit remain thin, and India still lacks an interoperable fuel card. A 1% card surcharge does not fit trucking margins ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/councils/forbesfinancecouncil/2025/10/28/payments-infrastructure-for-trucking-is-an-overlooked-opportunity-in-india/)).

### Compliance has moved from checkposts to clerical penalties

GST removed physical state checkposts but brought a new paperwork burden. Small errors can be costly: a tribunal had to quash a **₹10.28 lakh penalty** imposed only for a wrong vehicle number on an e-way bill ([TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/gstat-bengaluru-quashes-rs-10-28-lakh-penalty-wrong-vehicle-number-bill.html)). AIMTC has long complained that e-way bills expire while trucks queue to unload, which leads to seizure ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/industry/news/transporters-welcome-gst-council-move-to-curb-repeated-checks-on-highways-126100801507_1.html)). The GST Council's October 2026 decision to limit interceptions to written, intelligence-based checks and to stop routine checks in transit states is a real improvement. By mid-October only Tamil Nadu had implemented it, and the legislative amendments are still pending ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/economy/news/transit-states-can-check-e-way-bill-vehicles-only-on-specific-intelligence-126101201290_1.html); [A2Z Taxcorp](https://a2ztaxcorp.net/gst-big-update-cbic-implements-3-major-reforms-ahead-of-legislative-amendments-no-arrests-restricted-e-way-bill-checks-%E2%82%B910000-scn-threshold/)). Small goods transport agencies also face a harder tax choice. From September 2025 most sources say the forward-charge-with-credit option rose to 18%, which pushes small operators toward 5% options under which they cannot recover GST paid on trucks and tyres ([Lakshmikumaran & Sridharan](https://lakshmisri.com/newsroom/news-briefings/impact-of-gst-rate-changes-on-the-logistic-sector)). FASTag KYC and blacklisting rules have tightened, and the ₹3,000 annual pass excludes commercial vehicles ([NHAI FAQ](https://nhai.gov.in/nhai/sites/default/files/mix_file/FAQ_20_jun_25_Hindi.pdf)). In July 2025 Maharashtra truckers went on an indefinite strike against aggressive e-challan recovery ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/india/truckers-on-indefinite-strike-against-e-challans-in-maharashtra-supply-of-essentials-not-affected/2677852/)).

Bribery has moved rather than disappeared. The most recent national estimate is still pre-COVID: truckers paid **₹47,852 crore a year** in bribes, about ₹1,257 per trip, and 67% of drivers admitted paying police ([Business Standard, 2020](https://business-standard.com/article/current-affairs/truckers-pay-6-7-bn-in-bribes-a-year-same-as-arunachal-s-5-yr-infra-spend-120030900310_1.html)). The 2026 arrest of a GST assistant commissioner for a ₹1.5 lakh bribe to reduce an e-way bill penalty shows that rent-seeking now happens through mobile GST squads ([ETV Bharat](https://etvbharat.com/en/state/gst-officer-arrested-while-taking-bribe-in-kanker-jagdalpur-acb-team-caught-him-taking-rs-1-dot-5-lakh-enn26112106346)). Digitisation has reached compliance (e-way bills, FASTag, e-challans, and ULIP, the government's logistics data platform, which has handled over 200 crore API calls) faster than it has reached commercial documents. No independent data shows what share of lorry receipts (LRs) or PODs are digital, and no e-LR mandate exists ([Tribune India](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/business/india-closes-2025-with-strong-industrial-growth-startup-expansion-and-boost-in-investment-dpiit-year-end-review)).

## Roughly 40 drivers are missing for every 100 trucks

Driver supply limits capacity. Most 2025 estimates put availability at **55–60 drivers per 100 trucks** ([91trucks](https://www.91trucks.com/news/how-truck-drivers-keep-indias-economy-moving-every-day); [Nexdigm](https://www.nexdigm.com/market-research/insights/blog/road-freight-market-opportunity-analysis/)). Transport Minister Gadkari gave a higher figure of about 75 per 100 ([The Week/PTI](https://theweek.in/wire-updates/business/2025/01/28/dcm106-biz-ld-report-truck-drivers.html)). The gap between these figures probably reflects different methods. The causes are clear. In SaveLIFE Foundation's survey of 1,217 drivers, **53% earned ₹10,000–20,000 a month**, 93% had no social security, nine in ten had no formal training before getting a licence, and **84% would not recommend the job to family** ([Autocar Professional](https://www.autocarpro.in/news-national/most-truck-drivers-in-india-are-sleep-deprived-compromise-road-safety-savelifes%20hardhitting-report-55710)). Drivers average about 12 hours at the wheel a day, and half keep driving while fatigued. IIT Delhi's 2025 screening of 50,000 drivers found **55% had compromised vision and 57% elevated blood pressure** ([The Week/PTI](https://theweek.in/wire-updates/business/2025/01/28/dcm106-biz-ld-report-truck-drivers.html)). The driver shortage reaches shippers as cancelled bookings: a truck without a driver stays at origin.

Government responses are small relative to the problem. The **368 Apna Ghar rest stops with 4,611 beds** (July 2025) serve a workforce of millions ([DD India](https://ddindia.co.in/2025/08/govt-launches-apna-ghar-resting-facilities-for-truck-drivers-across-highways/)). Mandatory AC cabins apply only to N2/N3 trucks built from October 2025 ([Autocar Professional](https://www.autocarpro.in/News/govt-mandates-ac-cabins-for-trucks-manufactured-from-october-1-2025-118176)). The average long-haul truck is now about **10 years old**, a 20-year high, so most drivers will not get AC cabins for years. About **1.1 million trucks are over 15 years old** ([ITLN](https://itln.in/road-transportation/indias-ageing-truck-fleet-could-escalate-freight-spends-by-28-1355526)), and fewer than 3% of eligible vehicles have been scrapped ([S&P Global Mobility](https://www.spglobal.com/automotive-insights/en/blogs/2025/09/india-vehicle-scrappage-policy-insights)). An older fleet means more breakdowns and higher fuel and maintenance costs. Driver legal risk also directly threatens supply: the January 2024 strike against the 10-year hit-and-run provision emptied about **2,000 fuel pumps in two days** ([Tribune India](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/india/2k-fuel-stations-run-dry-as-truckers-protest-hit-run-law-govt-firefights-577754)).

Physical infrastructure has improved in motion but not at the dock. FASTag cut average toll waits from about 12 minutes to **47 seconds** ([News On Air](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/average-waiting-time-at-fee-toll-plazas-reduced-from-734-seconds-to-47-seconds-union-minister-nitin-gadkari-inform-ls)), and India rose to 38th in the World Bank's 2023 Logistics Performance Index ([PIB](https://pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1919925)). The constraint is now dwell time at shipper and consignee sites. A truck held a day at each end of a 2–3 day trip loses roughly 40% of its productive capacity. That is an inference from the cited dwell figures, but it shows that turnaround, not highway speed, sets effective fleet size.

## Platforms make money from truckers' spending, not from load matching

The commercial record shows what has and has not worked. **BlackBuck**, India's only listed trucking platform, turned profitable in FY26, with about **₹652 crore of operating revenue and ₹160 crore of profit** ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/business/blackbuck-posts-strong-q4-revenue-growth-fy26-profit-at-160-crore)). It earns about 98% from truck-operator services: FASTag, fuel cards and telematics. About 0.79 million operators transact with it each month ([Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/fintrackr/blackbuck-records-rs-29-cr-profit-on-rs-151-cr-revenue-in-q2-fy26-10624660)). Only recently has it added load classifieds, managed "SuperLoads" freight and lending. A new "truck hiring charges" cost line signals a move into managed brokerage, and its own loan book is still only about ₹60 crore ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/features/decoding-blackbucks-core-trucking-model-and-its-new-engines/)). **Porter** became a unicorn and turned profitable on **₹4,306 crore** of intra-city revenue, where order density is high and trips are short ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/porter-turns-profitable-in-fy25-revenue-crosses-inr-4000-cr-mark)).

The intercity load-matching and asset-heavy models have done badly. **Rivigo**, once a unicorn running relay trucking, lost about ₹542 crore in FY20 and sold its express unit to Mahindra Logistics for ₹225 crore, below that unit's annual turnover ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/mahindra-logistics-to-acquire-troubled-startup-rivigos-b2b-express-business)). Its revenue then fell about 85% ([TheKredible](https://thekredible.com/blogs/rivigos-revenue-dwindled-83-in-fy23-records-rs-91-cr-in-profits)). **Vahak**, the leading open load board, saw revenue fall 24% to ₹41 crore in FY24 and has raised no money since 2022 ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/company/vahak/financials/)). WheelsEye still lost about ₹47 crore on ₹243 crore of revenue ([Angel One](https://www.angelone.in/news/unlisted-companies/wheelseye-reports-modest-revenue-growth-losses-remain-steady-in-fy25)). FreightTiger, which sells freight software to enterprises, earns under ₹18 crore ([TheKredible](https://thekredible.com/blogs/freight-tiger-reports-rs-20-88-crore-revenue-in-fy24-losses-surge-by-77)). Funding for road-freight platforms fell about 70% from 2021 to 2022, and Freightwalla shut down ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/?p=418396)).

The reason these models failed is economic. A platform that pays truckers quickly has to finance the 40–45+ day shipper receivable, so intercity truckload brokerage is really a lending business carrying shipper credit risk. That makes it fragile when capital is expensive. The survivors built trust by paying quickly: Raaho pays 90% upfront and the balance within 24 hours of POD ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/?p=418396)). Adoption among truckers also needs on-ground support. Many operators lack smartphones or distrust apps ([Redseer](https://redseer.com/articles/unlocking-growth-in-indias-fragmented-trucking-sector/)), and drivers still prefer to deal with brokers face to face ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2025/12/bridging-the-gap-how-tender-truck-is-fixing-indias-broken-trucking-market)).

An open-protocol B2B freight play is therefore available but unproven.

## Conclusion

The common pitch of "Uber for trucks" misreads the problem. Matching loads to trucks is a small part of a broker's job. Brokers survive because they absorb counterparty risk and finance the gap between upfront trip costs and late shipper payment. Any challenger must take over those functions, not only the matching, and that makes intercity freight tech a credit and trust business that happens to use logistics software. BlackBuck's path supports this. It built a profitable base by handling truckers' spending on tolls and fuel, which gives it data for underwriting, and only then moved into loads and lending. The open question is whether payment data plus government data from ULIP, e-way bills and FASTag can prove that a trip actually happened well enough to release payment at delivery rather than 45 days later. If it can, the paper POD stops being the main source of delay, and much of the fraud, deduction dispute and broker dependence goes with it.

Three things remain uncertain. No one has measured broker share, no-show rates, digital POD penetration or fraud losses. The October 2026 e-way bill reform has not yet been adopted outside Tamil Nadu. And the official logistics cost figure of 7.97% of GDP shows that India's problem is unequal costs rather than uniformly high ones. The money to be made lies less in lowering the national average than in closing the gap between the ₹85,000 a well-connected shipper pays and the ₹1.5 lakh an MSME pays for the same lane.
