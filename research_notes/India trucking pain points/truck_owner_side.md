# Supply-Side Pain Points: Truck Owners, Small Fleet Operators and Transporters in India

Research note. About 16 search/fetch calls. Most hard numbers on Indian trucking come from consultancy blogs, platform and vendor content, or old government committee reports. Very few are primary measured datasets. Each source's quality is flagged below. Dates in [brackets] are publication dates where known.

## Market structure: how much of the fleet do small operators (1–5 trucks) own?

### Takeaway
The headline figure is that about 75% of truck operators own fewer than 5 trucks. It is widely repeated, from Redseer [Dec 2024] to Deloitte, but it goes back to an older NTDPC (National Transport Development Policy Committee, c. 2013–14) breakdown. Be careful to separate "share of operators" from "share of trucks": CRISIL [2018] says small operators are about 67% of operators but own less than 25% of heavy trucks (>12T).

### Cited Findings
- About 12.5 million trucks run by about 3.5 million operators, which is roughly 3.6 trucks per operator on average. About 75% of operators own fewer than 5 trucks [Redseer, 12 Dec 2024] — [Redseer](https://redseer.com/articles/unlocking-growth-in-indias-fragmented-trucking-sector/)
- Same Redseer piece: truck freight value was about $170 bn in FY24, with 8–9% CAGR expected to FY28. Digital freight penetration is below 2%. Road carries 46% of logistics activity (FY24). The used-CV financing market was $54–56 bn in FY24. Telematics penetration was 20% in FY21, expected at 40–45% by FY24 — [Redseer](https://redseer.com/articles/unlocking-growth-in-indias-fragmented-trucking-sector/)
- Business Standard (CRISIL SME tracker, 2017) attributes the split to the NTDPC report: about 75% of operators own fewer than 5 trucks, about 14% own under 20, and about 11% own more than 20 (older data) — [Business Standard](https://business-standard.com/article/economy-policy/crisil-sme-tracker-for-small-fleet-operators-time-to-hit-mainstream-road-117080800040_1.html)
- CRISIL Research [2018]: small fleet operators are about 67% of road transport operators but own less than 25% of trucks above 12T GVW (older data, and it contradicts the "75% of trucks" framing) — [CRISIL](https://www.crisil.com/en/home/our-analysis/reports/2018/11/road-turns-rough-for-small-fleet-operators.html)
- Deloitte says about 75% of trucks are owned by small fleet owners. This is a share of trucks, not operators, which conflicts with CRISIL — [Deloitte press release](https://www2.deloitte.com/in/en/pages/consumer-business/articles/road-logistics-india/press-release.html)
- Other estimates: "more than 80%" of operators own fewer than 5 trucks [Deccan Herald] — [Deccan Herald](https://deccanherald.com/business/indian-truckers-caught-on-a-pot-holed-road-to-growth-aspirations-1218737.html). ">70% of fleet ownership" sits with small operators [Nexdigm, 2026] — [Nexdigm](https://www.nexdigm.com/market-research/insights/blog/india-truck-aggregator-industry/)
- A DNA India report says small fleet operators own about 80% of India's roughly 1 crore (10 mn) trucks and are the most likely to default on loans (undated, likely older) — [DNA India](https://www.dnaindia.com/business/report-lenders-tighten-credit-to-truck-operators-as-defaults-rise-1888094)
- KPMG [Jan 2026] names "fragmented, small-fleet ownership" as a structural brake on efficiency, without a number — [KPMG India](https://kpmg.com/in/en/blogs/2026/01/logistics-costs-to-gdp-can-budget-2026-finally-move-the-needle.html)

### Inferences
- The safe phrasing is: "about 75% of operators own fewer than 5 trucks (NTDPC, repeated by Redseer 2024)". The share of trucks owned by small operators is disputed: under 25% of heavy trucks (CRISIL) versus about 75–80% of all trucks (Deloitte, DNA). The gap probably comes from the vehicle segment (heavy versus all goods vehicles) and the year.
- An average of about 3.6 trucks per operator (12.5 mn / 3.5 mn) confirms the extreme fragmentation, whichever definition is used.

### Gaps
- I found no recent (2023–26) primary survey that measures fleet-size distribution. VAHAN registration data by owner was not analysed. The NTDPC original was not fetched.

## Finding loads: brokers, commissions, empty running, idle time, detention

### Takeaway
Small operators depend heavily on brokers or commission agents for loads, but I found no reliable published broker-commission percentage. Empty return running is estimated at 25–40% (KPMG: 25–30%). Trucks sit idle for 24–48 hours waiting for a load (Redseer) and run only about 18–20 days a month. Detention at loading and unloading points is poorly compensated, around ₹1,000–1,500 a day, and no statute requires it to be paid.

### Cited Findings
- Trucks spend an average of 24–48 hours idle waiting for loads and operate only 18–20 days a month. Brokers charge "high commissions" (no percentage given) [Redseer, Dec 2024] — [Redseer](https://redseer.com/articles/unlocking-growth-in-indias-fragmented-trucking-sector/)
- 25–30% of return trips are empty. Congestion, toll queues and loading delays add 1–2 days per trip. Trucks lose 6–8 hours per trip to manual interventions despite FASTag and ULIP. Port dwell is 3–4 days versus a 1–2 day global norm. Detention and demurrage cost ₹20,000–40,000 per container [KPMG India, Jan 2026; author estimates, no underlying source] — [KPMG India](https://kpmg.com/in/en/blogs/2026/01/logistics-costs-to-gdp-can-budget-2026-finally-move-the-needle.html)
- Empty running is 28–43% for India, versus 13–29% for the US and 15–30% for the EU (secondary compilation) — [Taxguru](https://taxguru.in/?p=784869)
- About 35% of trucks in the FTL network return empty (industry estimate quoted on a vendor blog) — [Binary Semantics](https://www.binarysemantics.com/blogs/unlock-long-haul-fleet-profitability-beyond-fleet-size/)
- "Nearly 40%" of trucks run empty on return (promotional piece for the TruckGuru platform, Apr 2026; low reliability) — [NewsKarnataka](https://newskarnataka.com/india/why-40-of-trucks-in-india-run-empty-and-how-truckguru-is-fixing-this-billion-rupee-problem/18042026/)
- Freight-matching could remove an estimated ₹1.5–2 lakh crore of wasted cost (a Clickpost summary attributing it to KPMG) — [Clickpost](https://www.clickpost.ai/blog/logistics-cost-statistics-india)
- ICRA (via Deccan Herald): about 25–30% of trucks are idle at any given time (older; measures idle fleet, not empty km) — [Deccan Herald](https://deccanherald.com/business/indian-truckers-caught-on-a-pot-holed-road-to-growth-aspirations-1218737.html)
- Broker dependence: an industry white paper says truck owners are "hugely dependent" on commission agents for loads and utilisation — [Startup India white paper](https://www.startupindia.gov.in/content/dam/invest-india/Templates/public/White%20Paper_Logistics%20Sector.pdf). Trukky describes the broker as a commission agent connecting fragmented owners with transport contractors — [Trukky blog](https://www.trukky.com/blog/?p=1981)
- Broker pricing example (vendor blog): a Delhi–Jaipur broker quote of ₹36,000–40,000. Some brokers charge a flat commission plus discretionary "fuel adjustments" — [TruckGuru blog](https://truckguru.co.in/blog/?p=2848)
- Digital platform commission reportedly about 15–20% from customers and truckers combined (StartupTalky, older, unverified) — [StartupTalky](https://migrate.startuptalky.com/tag/trucking-industry)
- Detention norms: a free window of 2–4 hours at each end, then about ₹200–500 per hour (vendor blog) — [TruckGuru detention blog](https://truckguru.co.in/blog/?p=1867). Operators are often paid only ₹1,000–1,500 a day, "barely enough to cover fixed costs", and unpaid waiting is "silently borne by drivers and truck owners who have no recourse" (trade commentary) — [India Sea Trade News](https://indiaseatradenews.com/?p=106977). One platform's own slab is ₹1,500/day, rising to ₹3,000 after 7 days for MXL trucks — [FR8](https://www.fr8.in/specialHaltingTerms.html)
- Disputes over detention are common because neither side has reliable timestamps — [Fretron glossary](https://www.fretron.com/blog/glossary-detention-demurrage/)
- Anecdote: drivers waited several days outside a TAFE tractor plant in Tamil Nadu that had no storage space for the tyres they delivered (driver community blog, 2021) — [Driversduniya](https://driversduniya.blogspot.com/2021/08/warehouse-on-wheels-wow.html)
- Shriram Mobility Bulletin [Mar 2025]: truck rental demand showed "uncharacteristic sluggishness" (industrial slowdown, weak infrastructure demand) — [Autocar Pro](https://www.autocarpro.in/news/miles-to-empty-indias-trucking-sector-signals-economic-drag-125325)

### Inferences
- A defensible range is 25–40% empty return running, with KPMG's 25–30% as the most credible citation. Note that it is an author estimate.
- Combining 18–20 operating days a month, 24–48 hours between loads and 1–2 days of delay per trip, a typical small-fleet truck plausibly loses around a third of its available time to non-revenue activity. This is a synthesis, not a measured figure.
- Detention is largely unenforceable for small owners. They have no bargaining power, contracts are informal and there is no timestamp evidence, so the cost of waiting effectively shifts to them.

### Gaps
- I found no reliable figure for traditional broker or commission-agent cuts. Practitioner accounts often cite a few percent or a fixed amount per trip, but I found no source for this, so it is unverified.
- No government-measured empty-running statistic for 2023–26. NITI Aayog / RMI freight reports (2021) were not fetched.
- No systematic data on average detention hours per trip for Indian FTL.

## Pricing power and margins: diesel, tolls, low freight rates, undercutting

### Takeaway
Fuel plus tolls make up about 80% of operating costs (Redseer). Fuel alone is 30–55%, depending on the source. AIMTC says freight rates stayed flat in 2026 while diesel, tolls, insurance, tyres, AdBlue, finance and compliance costs rose, and warns that thousands of small and self-driving owners may shut down. Margins are single-digit.

### Cited Findings
- Toll and fuel payments are almost 80% of a typical operator's expenses [Redseer, Dec 2024] — [Redseer](https://redseer.com/articles/unlocking-growth-in-indias-fragmented-trucking-sector/)
- AIMTC [24 Jul 2026, Bal Malkit Singh]: freight rates are "largely unchanged" despite sharp rises in diesel, tolls, insurance, maintenance, tyres, AdBlue, finance and compliance costs. "Thousands of small transport operators and self-driving truck owners will be forced to shut down" without corrective action. Demands: toll rationalisation, a freight-rate mechanism tied to actual operating costs, withdrawal of mandatory VLTD (vehicle tracking device) and a fix for VAHAN integration glitches that cause downtime, plus parking and rest areas. The article gives no figures — [Free Press Journal](https://freepressjournal.in/mumbai/small-transport-operators-face-financial-crisis-as-rising-costs-and-stagnant-freight-rates-threaten-supply-chain-aimtc)
- AIMTC: "Fuel alone contributes nearly 55% of truck operating costs." AIMTC repeatedly asks for diesel under GST and "One Nation, One Tax, One Uniform Diesel Price" (2026) — [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/transporters-seek-immediate-diesel-price-cut-as-global-crude-falls-below-us75-per-barrel); see also [FPJ: 4th fuel hike in 10 days](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/4th-fuel-hike-in-10-days-pushes-transport-sector-to-brink-warns-aimtc-triggers-severe-financial-strain)
- AIMTC [Jan 2025, Harish Sabharwal] asked the government to address an "exorbitant increase in toll tax rates" and misuse of e-challans — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/india-news/aimtc-urges-government-to-address-issues-of-toll-hikes-e-challan-misuse-125012400954_1.html)
- Fuel is 30–50% of total opex. Largely owner-managed fleets run on single-digit margins. Fuel theft costs an estimated ₹25,000–52,000 per truck per month. Idling and detours waste 10–20% of fuel. Adulteration raises consumption 5–10%. The column mentions a 2026 crude spike from about $69 to above $150/bbl [Autocar Pro column by Repos Energy founder, 17 Aug 2026; vendor-authored] — [Autocar Pro](https://www.autocarpro.in/opinion-column/indias-commercial-vehicle-segment-runs-on-fuel-and-guesswork-134142)
- After the 2026 fuel hikes, transporters raised freight about 3–4%. AITWA guided a 3–3.5% rise depending on route and vehicle — [Kotak Neo](https://www.kotakneo.com/news/market-news/freight-rates-raised-4-percent-rising-transport-costs/); [India Shipping News](https://indiashippingnews.com/export-import/aitwa-expresses-concern-over-diesel-price-increase-freight-rates-likely-to-rise-by-3-to-3-5/). In Gujarat, transporters sought a 10% hike — [Gujarat Samachar](https://english.gujaratsamachar.com/news/gujarat/transporters-seek-10-freight-hike-amid-rising-fuel-prices-industries-fear-higher-production-costs-80397571326.html). SIMTA urged cuts in tolls and VAT — [Siasat](https://www.siasat.com/fuel-price-hike-sparks-concern-among-transporters-simta-urges-centre-to-cut-toll-vat-3479499/)
- A report says about 30% of the trucking fleet went off-road amid diesel price hikes (headline claim, unverified) — [NewsBytes](https://www.newsbytesapp.com/news/business/30-of-india-s-trucking-fleet-off-roads-amid-diesel-price-hike/story)
- Each 10% gain in utilisation is estimated to cut long-haul freight rates by 6–7% (KPMG author estimate) — [KPMG India](https://kpmg.com/in/en/blogs/2026/01/logistics-costs-to-gdp-can-budget-2026-finally-move-the-needle.html)
- Cash-flow squeeze: fuel is paid weekly but clients pay in 30–60 days. The columnist says this mismatch "has broken more small fleets than any single price hike" — [Autocar Pro](https://www.autocarpro.in/opinion-column/indias-commercial-vehicle-segment-runs-on-fuel-and-guesswork-134142). A banker says client payments that used to come in 30–45 days now take about 90 days, and smaller transporters defer EMIs (undated, likely older) — [DNA India](https://www.dnaindia.com/business/report-lenders-tighten-credit-to-truck-operators-as-defaults-rise-1888094). Transporters have demanded loan moratoria to avoid NPAs — [Mobility Outlook](https://mobilityoutlook.com/news/transporters-staring-at-npas-demands-moratorium-to-stay-afloat)
- Lenders offer challan or freight-invoice discounting, including to owners of 1–2 trucks — [Shriram Finance](https://www.shriramfinance.in/articles/business-loan/2025/how-challan-discounting-can-improve-cash-flow). Small operators with incomplete records face delays or lower eligibility for working capital — [IIFL](https://www.iifl.com/Blogs/business-loan/how-small-logistics-operators-use-fleet-data-to-secure-working-capital)

### Inferences
- Small owners are price-takers. With costs around 80% pass-through items (fuel and tolls) and only single-digit margins, a 10% diesel rise can wipe out the margin unless freight rises about 4–5%. Observed hikes of about 3–4% suggest only partial pass-through. This is an inference from the cited ranges.
- Oversupply (≈3.5 mn operators, low entry barriers through used-CV finance) plus broker-intermediated spot pricing is the likely cause of rate undercutting. I found no direct quantitative source on undercutting.

### Gaps
- No published per-km freight-rate index time series was fetched (IFTRT or the Shriram Mobility Bulletin would be the sources).
- No data on how much of a cost rise brokers or shippers absorb versus pass through.
- No quantified toll share on its own. The toll bill as % of opex was not found separately; Redseer combines it with fuel.

## Fraud and trust: fake loads, payment scams, advance-fee scams, cargo theft

### Takeaway
Advance-fee "fake truck/transporter" fraud is well documented in Indian police reporting, including a case run through the Vahak app. Most reported cases target shippers or small businesses booking trucks rather than truck owners. Fraud against truck owners is less documented: fake loads that demand a "booking fee", unpaid freight, and bounced cheques from intermediaries. Fraud B2B cases between logistics firms show large-ticket payment default.

### Cited Findings
- A 22-year-old was arrested for duping people through a logistics app (Vahak). A victim booked a truck and was asked for an ₹15,000 advance — [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/india/two-arrested-for-duping-people-with-fake-logistics-services-in-delhi/3058108/)
- In Delhi, two people were arrested for posing as representatives of established logistics firms and taking advance payments for truck bookings (Sept 2026) — [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/india/two-arrested-for-duping-people-with-fake-logistics-services-in-delhi/3058108/)
- A Delhi transporter was duped of ₹50,000 in a fake truck-hiring fraud; one person was held — [Ground News](https://ground.news/article/delhi-transporter-duped-of-rs-50-000-in-fake-truck-hiring-fraud-one-held)
- A man was held for cheating people on the pretext of providing trucks for goods transport — [Devdiscourse](https://www.devdiscourse.com/article/law-order/2101598-man-held-for-cheating-people-on-pretext-of-providing-truck-for-goods-transportation)
- A Panchkula businessman looking to transport rice to Kerala was duped of ₹1.99 lakh online — [Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/chandigarh/panchkula-businessman-looking-to-transport-rice-to-kerala-duped-of-rs-1-99-lakh-online/amp)
- A Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar transport company was cheated of ₹28.47 lakh by a Mumbai-based gang using fake firms (Apr 2025) — [Lokmat Times](https://www.lokmattimes.com/aurangabad/fake-firms-cheat-transport-company)
- Unizen Logistics filed an FIR against ULR Freight directors for ₹5.74 crore unpaid; the cheques bounced (Nov 2025) — [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-ulr-freight-directors-booked-for-allegedly-cheating-unizen-logistics-of-574-crore-in-payments)
- In an Indore WhatsApp fraud, a tile trader lost ₹25 lakh. The fraudsters hired a genuine third-party transporter and paid the driver a ₹5,000 token to load the goods, so transporters become unwitting instruments — [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/indore/5000-token-made-scam-look-real-indore-tile-trader-loses-25-lakh-in-whatsapp-fraud)
- Driver-side scam: fake orders with pickups at army stations or airports, where the driver partner is asked to send "advance money" to take the order (cybercrime blog, low reliability) — [Mover blog](https://mover.delivery/blog/latest-cyber-crime-tactics)
- Fake e-challan scams via WhatsApp APK links also target vehicle owners — [Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/himachal/challan-scam-alert-fraudsters-use-whatsapp-apk-links-to-loot-money)
- Vahak markets "fraud prevention" as a core feature, which implies fraud is a known problem on load boards (company-promotional) — [exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/advertising-news/vahak-drives-home-the-importance-of-financial-security-for-truckers-in-new-campaign-125885.html)

### Inferences
- The trust deficit runs both ways. Shippers fear fake transporters and owners fear fake loads or non-payment. Open classified-style load boards with low KYC let advance-fee fraud happen. This is a reason incumbents stress verification and escrow.

### Gaps
- No aggregate statistics on load-board fraud against truck owners. NCRB does not break this out.
- Cargo theft and highway robbery data for India (e.g., TAPA APAC) was not found within this session.
- Systematic app-store review mining (Vahak, BlackBuck, TruckSuvidha) was not done. Searches returned no Vahak-specific complaints.

## Experience with apps (BlackBuck, Vahak, TruckSuvidha, etc.): fees, subscriptions, complaints

### Takeaway
BlackBuck (Zinka Logistics, listed) earns about 98% of revenue from truck-operator services: FASTag, fuel cards, telematics subscriptions, a subscription-led "Loads Classifieds" marketplace and vehicle finance. It had about 7.65 lakh average monthly transacting operators in Q4 FY25. Vahak markets itself as free and 0% commission, monetising through membership and value-added services. Digital freight penetration is still below 2%.

### Cited Findings
- BlackBuck FY25 operating revenue was ₹427 crore (+44% YoY from ₹297 crore). Truck operator services were 98% of operating revenue — [Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/news/blackbuck-posts-rs-41-cr-pbt-in-q4-fy25-revenue-grows-31-9311660). Another report gives FY25 total revenue of ₹462.4 crore and a net loss of ₹8.7 crore — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/company/blackbuck/financials/)
- Average monthly transacting truck operators were 7.65 lakh in Q4 FY25 (+17.5% YoY) — [Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/news/blackbuck-posts-rs-41-cr-pbt-in-q4-fy25-revenue-grows-31-9311660)
- BlackBuck's model: fees on successful load matches, plus subscriptions for GPS, FASTag and fuel cards. Loads Classifieds is "subscription-led". A 6–8% commission per transaction on loads was cited, possibly rising over time — [Inc42 Q4 coverage](https://inc42.com/buzz/blackbuck-posts-inr-280-cr-profit-in-q4-on-tax-credit)
- Inc42 [9 Nov 2026] describes the revenue lines: bank commissions plus service fees on FASTag (issuance, replacement, recharge), OMC commissions on fuel cards, telematics subscriptions, SuperLoads service income and vehicle-finance fees and interest. It gives no price points. The pain points it lists are finding loads, negotiating rates, tolls, fuel tracking and financing — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/features/decoding-blackbucks-core-trucking-model-and-its-new-engines/)
- A ValuePickr investor forum estimates only about 10–15k operators use BlackBuck's fueling, loads and finance products, against hundreds of thousands on FASTag and telematics (forum analysis, unverified) — [ValuePickr](https://forum.valuepickr.com/t/zinka-logistics-accelerating-freight-on-digital-highway/185427)
- Vahak claims to be "completely free" and a "0% commission" load and lorry matching platform — [Vahak](https://www.vahak.in/mob/online-load-booking). It claims over 25 lakh transport MSMEs, shippers and fleet owners, with membership perks and VAS such as GPS, insurance and loans — [mediainfoline](https://www.mediainfoline.com/brand/vahak-profitseyaari-offers-shippers-carriers). It has a tie-up with AIMTC to digitise road logistics — [BW Autoworld](https://bwautoworld.com/article/vahak-teams-up-with-aimtc-to-digitalise-indian-road-logistics-495000)
- TrucksUp launched AI-driven fuel subscription plans for transporters — [ITLN](https://www.itln.in/road-transportation/trucksup-launches-first-ai-driven-fuel-subscription-plans-for-transporters-1358504)
- Digital freight penetration is below 2% [Redseer, Dec 2024] — [Redseer](https://redseer.com/articles/unlocking-growth-in-indias-fragmented-trucking-sector/)

### Inferences
- Rough calculation: ₹427 crore / 7.65 lakh operators ≈ ₹5,600 per operator per year, or about ₹470 a month. This suggests apps monetise operators mainly through payments and tolls and fuel float, not load matching. It is consistent with load matching still being mostly done offline by brokers.
- The real price of "free" or 0%-commission load boards is paid through memberships, lead-unlock fees or bundled VAS. The specific price points need verifying in the app stores.

### Gaps
- No verified current subscription prices for Vahak membership, BlackBuck Loads or TruckSuvidha. TruckSuvidha did not appear in the search results at all.
- No structured review analysis (Google Play ratings, common complaint themes such as fake leads, unresponsive loaders, paid plans not delivering loads). This needs manual app-store or Reddit mining.

## Truck utilisation (km/day) vs global benchmarks

### Takeaway
Indian trucks average about 250–300 km a day (KPMG 2026), against over 500 km in China and 700–800 km in the US. Other estimates put India at 250–450 km a day. Utilisation is depressed by empty returns, loading delays, checkpoints and only about 18–20 operating days a month.

### Cited Findings
- India 250–300 km/day, US 700–800 km/day, China over 500 km/day [KPMG India, Jan 2026] — [KPMG India](https://kpmg.com/in/en/blogs/2026/01/logistics-costs-to-gdp-can-budget-2026-finally-move-the-needle.html)
- India 250–400 km/day, BRICS peers about 500, US 700–800 — [Taxguru](https://taxguru.in/?p=784869)
- After GST [2018], the average truck rose to about 400–450 km/day once checkposts were removed (older) — [PressReader / FPJ (PTI)](https://www.pressreader.com/india/the-free-press-journal/20180101/282024737641466)
- An Indian truck runs about 50,000–60,000 km a year versus about 300,000 km in the US [Business Today, 2018; older, does not reconcile with daily figures] — [PressReader / Business Today](https://www.pressreader.com/india/business-today/20180506/282054802626107)
- A 2020 driver survey averaged about 417 km per driving day across cities (secondary source, older) — [Coda (Raaho market sizing)](https://coda.io/@aryan-kota/raaho-dd/market-sizing-9); see also [Statista](https://statista.com/statistics/1108993/india-average-distance-driven-by-truck-drivers-per-day-by-city)
- Trucks operate 18–20 days a month [Redseer, Dec 2024] — [Redseer](https://redseer.com/articles/unlocking-growth-in-indias-fragmented-trucking-sector/)

### Inferences
- The figures differ because of definition: "per driving day" (≈400+ km) versus "per calendar day including idle" (≈250–300 km). On a calendar-day basis Indian trucks are roughly a third of US utilisation. Annual km: 250–300 × 365 ≈ 90–110k km, versus 50–60k in 2018 data. That rise is a plausible post-GST and FASTag improvement, but it is not confirmed.

### Gaps
- No official MoRTH or NITI measured utilisation dataset for 2023–26 was found. The FASTag- or ULIP-derived average speed and transit-time data (e.g., IIM Calcutta / Transport Corporation of India studies) was not fetched in this session.
